Aid-in-dying & euthanasia laws worldwide

Two distinctions shape every law: whether the patient must self-administer the medication (assisted dying) or a clinician may administer it (euthanasia), and whether it is limited to the terminally ill or extends to non-terminal unbearable suffering.

The two dominant models

The North American model

Every US state, plus New Zealand and (largely) Australia: terminally ill, capable adults (usually ≤6 months), the patient must self-administer, and residency is generally required.

The European / Benelux model

The Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, Spain, and Canada: eligibility extends to non-terminal unbearable suffering, a clinician may administer (euthanasia), and the Netherlands and Belgium reach minors under strict safeguards.

Where New Mexico sits

Firmly in the restrictive North American model — terminal illness ≤6 months, self-administration only, NM residency required — but among the most access-oriented US laws in procedure: it permits trained NP/PA prescribers, a single request, and a shortened, waivable 48-hour waiting period.

If you are in crisis

Aid in dying is not for emotional or mental-health crisis. If you are in distress or thinking about harming yourself, please call or text 988 — the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, free and confidential, 24/7.
